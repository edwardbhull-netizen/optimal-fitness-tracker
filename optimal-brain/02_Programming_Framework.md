# OPTIMAL BRAIN: FILE 02. MASTER PROGRAMMING FRAMEWORK

This is the rulebook. File 01 explains where the ideas come from. File 03 is the exercise library. This file tells the AI exactly HOW to build a programme for an Optimal member, step by step, with the decisions a master coach with 20+ years of experience would make.

Optimal context: private members coaching facility, adults mostly 40+, including former athletes, parents who've become inactive, people dealing with menopause and joint issues. Goals: longevity, strength, better movement, posture, energy, fat loss, quality of life. Formats: Groups (larger classes, one session for mixed abilities), Teams (small group, individualised programmes, close to PT), plus Golf and Seniors programmes. Everyone starts with a 28 day kickstart.

Equipment available (confirm against the brain's existing equipment rules, which take priority): barbells, safety squat bar, plates, benches, kettlebells, dumbbells, rig with pull up bars, TRX/suspension trainers, cable machine, sled, ski erg, rower, assault bike, wall balls / medicine balls, ab mats, mats, bands, boxes/steps, plus other small kit.

---

## SECTION 0. THE AI'S DECISION ORDER (follow every time)

When asked to build or edit a programme:

1. **Safety screen**: any red flags, medical conditions, injuries, pain, recent surgery? If red flags (Section 12.1) → do not programme that area, recommend referral, programme around it.
2. **Goal**: primary goal (strength, fat loss, muscle, mobility/pain, golf performance, general health/longevity) and any secondary goal.
3. **Format**: Group, Team, Golf, Seniors, or individual Teams programme. Session length and frequency.
4. **Training age and level**: beginner (under 6 months consistent), intermediate (6 months to 3 years), advanced (3+ years). Assign a grade (G1 to G4) per movement pattern using the screens (Section 3).
5. **Readiness**: traffic light (Section 2).
6. **Phase**: where they are in the 4 week block and the 12 week cycle (Section 10).
7. **Choose patterns** for the session/week so the weekly checklist is covered (Section 4).
8. **Choose exercises** from File 03 at the right level, respecting injuries and equipment.
9. **Set loading**: sets, reps, tempo, rest, RPE (Section 7).
10. **Set progression rule** for each exercise (Section 8) so the coach knows what to do next session.
11. **Add regression and progression options** for each exercise (from File 03) so the coach can adjust live.
12. **Output** in the standard format (Section 14).

Never: programme exercises the member has a contraindication for; use true 1RM testing for general members; prescribe training to failure on complex barbell lifts; skip the warm up; leave out pulling, single leg or carry work across a week.

## SECTION 1. INFORMATION THE AI NEEDS ABOUT A MEMBER

Minimum: name, age, sex, goal, programme type (group/team/golf/seniors), sessions per week, injuries/pain history, medical conditions, training experience.
Ideal (from assessment): screen results per pattern (Section 3), current working weights or rep maxes, level per pattern, preferences and dislikes, lifestyle (job, sleep, stress), menopause status where relevant and volunteered, golf handicap / goals for golf members.

If key info is missing, the AI should make the safest reasonable assumption (start one level lower) and flag what the coach should check.

## SECTION 2. READINESS: THE TRAFFIC LIGHT SYSTEM

Ask (or the coach records) before each session: sleep (good/ok/poor), energy (1 to 5), stress (1 to 5), soreness (1 to 5), pain anywhere (yes/no and 0 to 10), for women where volunteered: cycle/menopause symptoms affecting today.

| Light | Criteria | Action |
|---|---|---|
| GREEN | Slept well, energy 4 to 5, no new pain | Train as planned. Allowed to push top sets (RPE 8 to 9). |
| AMBER | Poor sleep OR energy 2 to 3 OR soreness 4+ OR mild pain (1 to 3/10) | Keep exercises, reduce load 10 to 20% or drop 1 set per exercise, cap RPE at 7, swap any painful exercise for its regression. Conditioning moderate not maximal. |
| RED | Multiple ambers, illness, pain 4+/10, very high stress, hormonal symptoms severely affecting them | "Working in" session: extended mobility, breathing, light technique work (RPE 5 to 6), walking/zone 2. Or rest. |

Pain monitoring rule (used by physios and adopted here): during exercise, pain up to 3/10 that does not increase as the session goes on and settles within 24 hours is generally acceptable for training around niggles. Above that, regress. Sharp, worsening, or lingering pain → stop that exercise and refer if persistent.

## SECTION 3. ASSESSMENT AND LEVEL ASSIGNMENT

Purpose: decide each member's starting grade (G1 to G4) for each pattern and spot priorities. Do it at the start of the 28 day kickstart and retest every 4 to 8 weeks. Coach level screening only, not diagnosis.

### 3.1 Quick screens (10 to 15 minutes)

| Screen | How | What poor looks like | Priority fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bodyweight squat to box | Arms forward, sit to a standard bench height box, stand | Heels lift, knees cave, big forward lean, can't reach box, pain | Ankle/hip mobility, goblet squat to box, heel elevated squats |
| Knee to wall (ankle) | Half kneeling, toes ~10cm from wall, knee to wall without heel lift | Can't reach or big L/R difference | Ankle mobs, calf work, slant board, tib raises |
| Split squat | Bodyweight, 5 reps each side | Loss of balance, knee caving, pain | Supported split squat, balance work |
| Single leg step down | 20cm step, slow lower, 5 each side | Knee caves, hip drops, trunk leans | Glute med work, Poliquin/Patrick step, lateral band walks |
| Hip hinge (dowel) | Dowel on back (head, upper back, sacrum contact), hinge to mid shin | Loses dowel contact, squats it, can't feel hamstrings | Hinge patterning, KB deadlift from blocks |
| Push up | Hands on bench or floor, chest to fist height | Hips sag, elbows flare wide, can't do 5 | Incline push up level selection |
| TRX row / inverted row | Body angle that allows 8 controlled reps | Shrugging, hips sag | Row level selection |
| Overhead reach (back to wall) | Back and head against wall, arms up to touch wall | Can't reach without back arching | Lats/T spine mobility, landmine press instead of overhead |
| Plank | Forearm plank to form failure (max 60s) | Under 20s or sagging | Core level selection |
| Side plank | Each side (max 45s) | Under 15s or big L/R difference | Side plank level |
| Single leg balance | Eyes open, 30s each side; then eyes closed | Under 10s eyes open (fall risk flag for older adults) | Balance training every session |
| Floor to stand | Sit on floor, stand up with minimal support | Needs lots of support / can't | Floor transfer progressions (get up ladder) |
| Grip / carry | Farmers carry with moderate KBs, 30m | Grip fails quickly, posture collapses | Carries every session |
| Conditioning | e.g. 500m row time, or 3 min bike at steady pace talk test | — | Sets conditioning zone baseline |

Optional tests for Teams: 6 to 10 rep max on key lifts (trap bar deadlift, goblet/SSB squat, DB bench, TRX row), dead hang time, 30 second sit to stand count (seniors), 4 to 6 minute walk/row distance.

### 3.2 Grade definitions (Optimal's G1 to G4 system, applied per pattern)
- **G1 Foundation**: beginner or modified, supported or assisted, lowest load. (E.g. TRX assisted squat, box squat.)
- **G2 Standard**: the standard version with moderate load, owns the pattern. (Goblet squat.)
- **G3 Advanced**: increased load or complexity: single arm, single leg, paused, unstable, heavier DB/KB. (Paused goblet squat, double KB front squat.)
- **G4 Maximum**: maximum challenge: heavy barbell/SSB, advanced single leg, high skill power. Mostly Teams, coach approved. (SSB squat, back squat, pistol variations.)

A member can be G4 in hinge and G2 in single leg. Programme each pattern at its own grade.

### 3.2a Link to the existing Optimal onboarding screen
Optimal already screens every new member across 8 areas, each graded 1 to 3 (squat, single leg/step up, hinge, horizontal pull/TRX row at 45 degrees, vertical push/supported shoulder press, plank and side plank, Swiss ball hamstring curl, 3 minute bike test). Use those results to set the starting G grade: screen grade 1 → start at G1, screen grade 2 → G2, screen grade 3 → G2 to G3 (G3 if the coach is happy with technique under load). The quick screens in 3.1 add detail where needed (ankle, overhead reach, balance, floor to stand) and feed the corrective choices.

### 3.3 Progress to next level when
- Can complete the target reps for all sets with good form at RPE ≤7 for 2 consecutive sessions, AND
- No pain, AND
- Coach signs off technique.

## SECTION 4. MOVEMENT PATTERN SYSTEM AND WEEKLY CHECKLIST

### 4.1 Pattern categories (the brain's master list)
1. **Squat** (knee dominant, bilateral): goblet, SSB, front, box squats, wall balls.
2. **Hinge** (hip dominant): deadlift variations, RDL, swing, hip thrust, good morning, back extension.
3. **Single leg / Lunge**: split squat, RFESS, lunges, step ups, lateral lunge, single leg RDL (also hinge).
4. **Horizontal push**: push ups, bench, DB press, cable press.
5. **Vertical push**: overhead press, landmine press, half kneeling press.
6. **Horizontal pull**: rows (TRX, DB, cable, chest supported, barbell).
7. **Vertical pull**: pull up, chin up, lat pulldown (cable), band assisted pull ups.
8. **Core: anti extension**: plank, dead bug, rollout, body saw.
9. **Core: anti rotation**: Pallof press, bird dog, renegade/plank row, single arm carries.
10. **Core: anti lateral flexion**: side plank, suitcase carry, Copenhagen.
11. **Rotation / twist** (controlled power): woodchops, med ball rotational throws, landmine rotation.
12. **Loaded carry / gait**: farmers, suitcase, rack, overhead, sled.
13. **Power / plyometric**: med ball throws, jumps, swings, sled sprints, Olympic derivatives.
14. **Knee/ankle resilience (ATG)**: tib raises, calf raises, Patrick step, backward sled, Spanish squat, reverse Nordic.
15. **Shoulder health / upper back**: face pulls, band pull aparts, external rotation, Y-T-W, wall slides.
16. **Mobility**: hips, T spine, ankles, shoulders.
17. **Balance / floor transfer**: single leg balance, get up progressions, crawling.
18. **Conditioning**: intervals and steady state on bike, ski, rower, sled; MRT circuits.

### 4.2 Weekly minimum checklist (per member, across all their sessions)
- Squat ×1+, Hinge ×1+, Single leg ×2+, Horizontal push ×1+, Vertical push ×1 (or landmine), Horizontal pull ×2+, Vertical pull ×1+
- Core: each of anti extension, anti rotation, anti lateral flexion at least once
- Carry ×2
- Power: at least once per week (appropriate level)
- Knee/ankle resilience: 2× (in warm ups)
- Shoulder health/upper back: 2× (warm ups or accessories)
- Balance: every session for 60+ members; 2× for others
- Conditioning: 2 to 3 exposures (finishers count)
- Pull to push ratio: approx 2:1 sets for most desk based or rounded posture members (minimum 1:1).

### 4.3 Weekly splits by frequency
- **2 sessions/week**: 2 × full body (A/B).
- **3 sessions/week**: 3 × full body (A/B/C) — preferred for most members.
- **4 sessions/week**: Upper/Lower × 2, or 4 × full body with varied emphasis (strength / metabolic / strength / power+conditioning).
- **5+ (group heavy members)**: alternate higher and lower intensity days; ensure 1 to 2 easier days; avoid heavy same pattern on consecutive days.

## SECTION 5. SESSION STRUCTURE

### 5.1 Optimal standard session (45 minutes)
| Block | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Arrival + readiness | 2 min | Traffic light check |
| 2. Warm up (RAMP) | 8 to 10 min | Raise (bike/ski/row/backward walk), Activate/Mobilise (targeted drills), Potentiate (pattern rehearsal) |
| 3. Core + Power | 5 min | Core (anti movement) paired with power (med ball throws, swings, sled; jumps for screened Teams members only) |
| 4. Strength A | 10 to 12 min | Main lift paired with a non competing exercise (A1/A2) |
| 5. Strength B | 10 to 12 min | Secondary lifts as pair or tri set (B1/B2/B3) |
| 6. Finisher / conditioning | 5 to 8 min | Intervals, carries, sled, MRT |
| 7. Cool down | 2 to 3 min | Breathing, mobility |

### 5.2 Teams session (60 minutes) adds: longer strength blocks (two 15 min blocks), an accessory block (C1/C2/C3), and individual corrective work in the warm up.

### 5.3 Group session for mixed abilities (how to make one session work for everyone)
- Programme by PATTERN, then provide 3 to 4 level options for each slot (e.g. "Squat: G1 TRX assisted box squat / G2 goblet squat / G3 paused goblet or double KB front squat / G4 SSB squat").
- Use TIME or ROUNDS rather than fixed reps where possible (e.g. 40s work / 20s rest) so everyone finishes together.
- Use stations with kit that scales (KBs, TRX, cable, sled, bike).
- Coach cues on screen/whiteboard by level.
- Fixed rep schemes for strength blocks: give rep ranges (8 to 12) and RPE targets so each person picks their own load.

### 5.3a Optimal group session rules (BURN / STRONG / HYBRID)
These rules already exist in the Optimal brain and take priority over anything in Files 01 to 03:
- 45 minute sessions, groups of 4 rotating through stations, up to 24 people.
- **No jumping or high impact movements of any kind in group sessions.** Intensity comes from range of motion, load, volume, tempo and density. File 03 marks every exercise Group: Yes/No and gives a no impact swap for anything with impact (e.g. box jump → fast step up or sled push; burpee → step back burpee; jumping jacks → step jacks; skater jumps → lateral lunge or sled lateral drag).
- **Every movement has four graded variations, G1 to G4**, so mixed abilities and common limitations (knees, backs, shoulders) can train together.
- **BURN** (Mon/Fri): metabolic, high reps, short rest, continuous movement, always a max effort challenge (e.g. max calories bike/ski, scored AMRAP).
- **STRONG** (Tue): resistance focused, controlled tempo, compound movements, strength first.
- **HYBRID** (Wed): alternates cardio and resistance, should feel different every week.
- Warm ups always cover posture, hips, hamstrings and rotation.
- Every session gets a memorable name (e.g. "999 Call The Police" = three 9 minute AMRAPs).
- Jumps, hops, bounds, sprinting and Olympic lifts from the barbell are Teams only, after screening, with no pelvic floor symptoms and no osteoporotic fracture history.

### 5.4 Pairing rules (supersets/alternating sets)
- Pair non competing patterns: lower + upper, push + pull, hinge + push, squat + pull, single leg + core.
- Don't pair two grip heavy exercises (e.g. heavy deadlift + heavy row + farmers).
- Don't pair two exercises that both load the lower back heavily (e.g. RDL + bent over row) for 40+ members — use chest supported rows with hinges.
- Put the most technical/heaviest lift first in the session when fresh.
- Power always before strength; strength before metabolic.

## SECTION 6. WARM UP SYSTEM (RAMP)

**Raise (2 to 3 min)**: assault bike, ski erg, rower easy; or backward walking / backward sled drag (knee days); skipping; lunge matrix walking.
**Activate and Mobilise (4 to 5 min)**: choose 3 to 5 drills based on the day's patterns and the member's screen:
- Lower body day: tib raises ×15 to 25, knee to wall ankle rocks ×10 each, 90/90 hip switches ×6 each, glute bridge ×10, lateral band walk ×10 each, world's greatest stretch ×3 each, cossack squat ×5 each (Limber 11 elements).
- Upper body day: band pull aparts ×15, wall slides ×10, open book T spine ×6 each, scap push ups ×10, band external rotation ×12, face pull ×12 (Simple 6 elements).
- Back history: McGill Big 3 (curl up, side plank, bird dog) 6/4/2 reps with 8 to 10s holds, cat cow ×6, hip hinge drill ×8.
**Potentiate (2 min)**: pattern rehearsal with light load (2 lighter sets of the first lift), or low level power (med ball chest pass ×5, KB swing ×8; pogo hops or box jumps only for screened Teams members, never in groups).

Sample default warm ups:
- **Lower A**: 2 min backward walk/sled → tib raise ×20 → 90/90 ×5 each → glute bridge ×10 → goblet squat prying ×5 → world's greatest stretch ×3 each.
- **Upper A**: 2 min ski erg → band pull apart ×15 → wall slide ×10 → open book ×6 each → push up plus ×8 → face pull ×12.
- **Full body**: 2 min bike → lunge matrix ×2 each direction → inchworm to world's greatest stretch ×4 → bird dog ×6 each → band pull apart ×15 → KB deadlift ×8.

## SECTION 7. LOADING PARAMETERS BY GOAL

RPE scale: 10 = absolute max; 9 = 1 rep left; 8 = 2 left; 7 = 3 left; 6 = 4+ left. RIR = reps in reserve.

| Goal / quality | Sets per exercise | Reps | Tempo (Poliquin) | Rest | RPE | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Technique / learning | 2 to 3 | 6 to 10 | 3-1-1-1 | 60 to 90s | 5 to 6 | Never to fatigue |
| Stability / endurance (phase 1) | 2 to 3 | 12 to 15 | 3-1-1-0 or 4-0-1-0 | 45 to 60s | 6 to 7 | Accumulation |
| Hypertrophy (muscle) | 3 to 4 | 8 to 12 (6 to 20 range) | 3-0-1-0 / 3-1-1-1 | 60 to 120s | 7 to 9 | 10+ hard sets per muscle/week |
| Strength (general pop) | 3 to 5 | 4 to 8 | 3-1-1-0 / 3-1-X-0 | 2 to 3 min | 7 to 8 | Intensification |
| Max strength (advanced Teams only) | 4 to 6 | 3 to 5 | 2-1-X-0 | 3 to 4 min | 8 to 9 | Rep max on rotating variation (DeFranco) |
| Power | 3 to 5 | 3 to 6 | Explosive | 60 to 120s | Quality focus | Stop when speed drops |
| Muscular endurance / MRT | 2 to 4 rounds | 12 to 20 or 30 to 45s | Controlled | 15 to 30s between, 1 to 2 min between rounds | 7 | Fat loss / conditioning |
| Bone health (screened) | 3 to 5 | 5 or fewer | Controlled | 2 to 3 min | 8 (heavy, ~80 to 85% when progressed) | Progress gradually, supervised (LIFTMOR principle) |
| Tendon / rehab isometrics | 4 to 5 | 30 to 45s holds | Isometric | 1 to 2 min | ~70% effort | Pain relief and tendon loading (e.g. Spanish squat, wall sit) |
| Accessory / isolation | 2 to 3 | 10 to 20 | 2-1-1-1 | 45 to 75s | 8 to 9 | Can approach failure safely |

Weekly volume targets per major muscle group (hard sets): beginners 6 to 10, intermediate 10 to 16, advanced 12 to 20. 40+ members recover better at the lower to middle end; spread across 2 to 3 sessions.

Load selection without testing: pick a weight that hits the target reps at the target RPE. If they hit the top of the rep range at RPE ≤7, go heavier next set/session.

Estimating 1RM if needed: weight × (1 + reps/30) (Epley) using a set of 3 to 10 reps.

## SECTION 8. PROGRESSION MODELS (how every exercise moves forward)

The AI must attach ONE progression model to each exercise in a programme.

### 8.1 Double progression (default for most exercises)
Rep range e.g. 8 to 12. Start at a weight allowing 8 reps for all sets. Each session add reps. When all sets reach 12 with good form at RPE ≤8, increase load (2.5 to 5% or the next DB/KB increment) and return to 8.

### 8.2 Total reps progression (DeFranco repetition method)
Same weight, 3 sets, record total reps (e.g. 15+12+10 = 37). Next session beat the total. When total exceeds the target (e.g. 45), increase weight. Great for push ups, TRX rows, pull ups, DB presses, KB swings.

### 8.3 Linear load progression (beginners, main lifts)
Same sets × reps, add small load each week (lower body +2.5 to 5kg, upper body +1 to 2.5kg) while RPE stays ≤8. When it stalls twice, switch to double progression or wave.

### 8.4 RPE / autoregulated progression
Prescribe e.g. "3 × 6 at RPE 7 to 8". Load floats with daily readiness. Ideal for 40+ with variable sleep/stress/hormones.

### 8.5 Tempo / TUT progression (Poliquin)
Keep load, slow the eccentric (3s → 4s → 5s) or add pauses, before adding load. Ideal for beginners and joint sensitive members.

### 8.6 Range of motion progression (ATG / rehab)
Increase depth gradually (box height lowered, deficit added, heel elevation reduced) as long as pain free.

### 8.7 Level progression (Boyle / Cosgrove)
Move up the ladder in File 03 when the level criteria (Section 3.3) are met.

### 8.8 Density progression (Cosgrove)
Same work in less time, or more rounds in the same time (e.g. EMOM, AMRAP 10 min). For conditioning and MRT.

### 8.9 Wave / rep max progression (advanced Teams)
Rotate a main lift variation every 3 weeks; aim to beat a 5 to 8 rep max. Or waves e.g. 8-6-4 / 8-6-4 with increasing load.

### 8.10 Conditioning progression
Increase work duration → reduce rest → increase intensity (watts/pace) → increase rounds. Change only one at a time.

### 8.11 Rules for progressing
- Progress ONE variable at a time.
- Typical order for 40+: technique → range → reps → tempo/TUT → load → complexity/instability → speed/power.
- Never progress a painful exercise.
- If a member stalls 2 sessions in a row: check readiness, technique, sleep/nutrition; consider variation change or deload.

## SECTION 9. REGRESSION LOGIC AND PAIN DECISION TREE

### 9.1 Ways to regress any exercise (choose the smallest change that works)
1. Reduce load.
2. Reduce range (raise the box, use blocks, partial range).
3. Add support/assistance (TRX, rig, bench, band assistance).
4. Change stance to more stable (split → staggered → bilateral; single leg → supported).
5. Change implement (barbell → SSB/trap bar → DB/KB → cable/band → bodyweight).
6. Change angle (floor push up → incline; vertical press → landmine → incline press).
7. Slow the tempo / add isometric holds (reduces load needed, often reduces pain).
8. Reduce complexity (combined movement → separate parts).
9. Swap to the same pattern via a different exercise that avoids the painful position.
10. Remove impact (jumps → med ball throw → step ups / sled).

### 9.2 Pain decision tree
1. Pain during an exercise? → rate it 0 to 10.
2. 0 to 3/10, stable, settles after → continue, monitor, consider tempo/range adjustment.
3. 4+/10, or worsening, or sharp/catching/pins and needles → stop exercise.
4. Try a regression in this order: reduce range → change implement/angle → isometric version → different exercise for same pattern.
5. If no pain free version of the pattern → skip that pattern today, train everything else, log it.
6. Pain persists over 48 hours, recurrent, or red flags → refer to physio/GP (Optimal referral partners) and programme around it.

### 9.3 Common substitution rules
| Painful / unavailable | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Back squat | SSB squat, goblet squat, front squat, box squat, leg press-like belt squats not available → split squat |
| Conventional deadlift | Trap bar (if available) → KB deadlift from blocks → RDL → hip thrust / cable pull through |
| Barbell bench | DB bench neutral grip → floor press → push up → cable press → landmine press |
| Overhead press | Landmine press → half kneeling landmine → incline DB press → high incline press |
| Pull ups | Band assisted → eccentric only → lat pulldown (cable) → TRX high row / half kneeling single arm pulldown |
| Lunges (knee pain) | Reverse lunge → split squat (short range) → step up (low) → Spanish squat → sled backward drag |
| Running/jumping | Sled push, bike, ski, rower; med ball throws for power |
| Planks (shoulder/wrist pain) | Dead bug, Pallof press, bird dog, ab mat curl up (McGill), carries |
| Burpees | Squat thrust to incline, wall balls, sled, bike |
| Wrist pain push up | DB handles / push up bars, fist push up, cable press |

## SECTION 10. PERIODISATION: THE OPTIMAL 12 WEEK CYCLE

4 week blocks to match the 28 day rhythm. Each block: weeks 1 to 3 build, week 4 consolidate/deload or test.

| Block | Focus | Reps | Tempo | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Block 1 (wks 1 to 4) "Foundation / Accumulation" | Movement quality, work capacity, tissue tolerance | 10 to 15 | 3-1-1-0 | Learn patterns, find levels, build habit. The 28 day kickstart lives here. |
| Block 2 (wks 5 to 8) "Build / Hypertrophy-Strength" | Muscle and strength | 8 to 12 main, 10 to 15 accessories | 3-0-1-0 | Add load, progress levels, introduce more power |
| Block 3 (wks 9 to 12) "Strength / Intensification" | Strength and power | 4 to 8 main, 8 to 12 accessories | 3-1-X-0 | Heavier, more power, retest week 12 |
| Then repeat with new exercise variations at higher levels. |

Within each week 1 to 3: add reps or load per the progression model. Week 4 options:
- **Deload**: same exercises, ~50 to 60% volume (fewer sets), RPE ≤6 to 7. Use when members are beat up, or every 4th to 6th week for 50+ members.
- **Test/consolidate**: retest screens and rep maxes, celebrate PBs (great for retention and back end conversations).

Exercise rotation: keep main lifts for one 4 week block (beginners can keep for 8 weeks); change accessories every block; rotate main variations each block for intermediates/advanced (Poliquin/DeFranco principle) while keeping the pattern.

Deload triggers (autoregulated): 2+ weeks of stalled performance, persistent amber/red readiness, rising aches, poor sleep, life stress spikes, holidays.

## SECTION 11. CONDITIONING SYSTEM

### 11.1 Zones (talk test + RPE; use heart rate if available)
- **Zone 1 Recovery**: RPE 2 to 3, can sing.
- **Zone 2 Aerobic base**: RPE 3 to 4, can hold a full conversation but slightly breathy. 20 to 45 min. Builds mitochondria, fat oxidation, longevity (Attia).
- **Zone 3 Tempo**: RPE 5 to 6, short sentences.
- **Zone 4 Threshold**: RPE 7 to 8, few words.
- **Zone 5 VO2 max / sprint**: RPE 9 to 10.

### 11.2 Interval menu (bike, ski, rower, sled)
| Name | Protocol | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Aerobic intervals | 4 to 6 × 3 min at Zone 3/4, 1 to 2 min easy | Building engine, beginners |
| VO2 intervals | 4 to 5 × 3 to 4 min hard (RPE 8 to 9), equal rest | Longevity, fitness (Teams, 1×/week max) |
| Short sprints (Sims style) | 6 to 10 × 10 to 20s all out, 1 to 2 min easy | Menopause friendly, power, fat loss |
| 30/30 | 10 to 20 × 30s hard / 30s easy | General fat loss |
| Tabata style | 8 × 20s / 10s | Advanced, short finisher only |
| EMOM | e.g. bike calories every minute + rest | Group scaling (calorie targets by level) |
| Sled | 6 to 10 × 20m push, walk back recovery | Low impact, low soreness, all levels |
| Mixed modal | Row 250 / KB swing 10 / sled 20m × 4 rounds | MRT finisher |

Scaling in groups: give calorie/distance targets by level, or use time. Example assault bike targets for 15s sprint: G1 4 to 5 cal, G2 6 to 7, G3 8 to 9, G4 10+.

### 11.3 Weekly conditioning dose
Fat loss: 2 to 3 interval/MRT exposures + daily steps (8 to 10k target where possible). Longevity: 2 zone 2 sessions (can be outside the gym: walking, cycling) + 1 VO2 session. Seniors: steady intervals, no all out sprints until progressed, prefer bike/rower.

### 11.4 Metabolic resistance training (MRT) templates
- **Circuit A (beginner)**: Goblet squat 10, TRX row 10, KB deadlift 10, incline push up 8, farmers carry 30m — 3 rounds, 30s rest between exercises.
- **Circuit B (intermediate)**: DB reverse lunge 8 each, DB push press 8, KB swing 15, renegade row 6 each, ski erg 15 cal — 3 to 4 rounds, minimal rest, 90s between rounds.
- **Complex (advanced)**: DB RDL 6, DB hang clean 6, DB front squat 6, DB push press 6, DB reverse lunge 6 each — don't put down, 3 to 5 rounds.

## SECTION 12. SPECIAL POPULATIONS AND CONDITIONS (coach scope)

### 12.1 Red flags: refer before training that area / at all
Unexplained weight loss, night pain that doesn't change with position, chest pain or breathlessness disproportionate to effort, dizziness/fainting, numbness/weakness spreading in limbs, loss of bladder/bowel control or saddle numbness (urgent medical), recent trauma with possible fracture, hot swollen joint, uncontrolled blood pressure (e.g. resting over ~160/100), recent surgery without clearance, pregnancy complications. Coaches do not diagnose. Train around, refer, and use Optimal's physio/chiro/osteo/massage referral partners.

### 12.2 Knee pain (general, non acute, e.g. patellofemoral pain, mild osteoarthritis)
- Principles: load the knee in a tolerable way, build quads (especially with tempo and isometrics), glutes and calves; adjust range not activity entirely (Ben Patrick, Squat University, Poliquin).
- Warm up: backward walk/sled 3 min, tib raises, calf raises.
- Use: Spanish squat isometric (pain relief), wall sit, sled push/drag, step ups/Patrick step in pain free range, box squats at a pain free height, split squats short range, hamstring curls (Swiss ball/cable), hip thrusts.
- Avoid initially: deep loaded squats, jumping, lunges with big forward knee shear if painful.
- Progress depth gradually (8.6). Most knee OA improves with strength training.

### 12.3 Low back pain (non specific, chronic)
- Principles: McGill Big 3 daily, hip hinge retraining, walking, avoid loaded spinal flexion and twisting, build gradually.
- Use: dead bug, bird dog, side plank, Pallof press, suitcase carry, KB deadlift from blocks, hip thrusts, glute bridges, split squats (less spinal load than bilateral squats), chest supported rows, goblet squat.
- Avoid early: heavy barbell back squat, conventional deadlift from floor, sit ups/crunches, Russian twists, Jefferson curls, good mornings, toes to bar.
- Disc history: avoid loaded flexion and morning flexion; extension based mobility often feels better (cobra) but individualise.

### 12.4 Shoulder pain (impingement type, rotator cuff tendinopathy, stiffness)
- Principles: more pulling, upper back strength, rotator cuff strength, controlled overhead range (Cressey, Poliquin structural balance).
- Use: landmine press, neutral grip DB press, cable press, face pulls, band pull aparts, external rotations (cable/band), TRX rows, wall slides, scap push ups.
- Avoid early: barbell overhead press, behind the neck anything, wide grip dips, kipping, upright rows, painful end range.
- Frozen shoulder: gentle range work within tolerance, refer.

### 12.5 Hip pain / tightness (incl. mild OA)
- Use: 90/90 mobility, glute bridges/hip thrusts, side lying and banded abduction, Copenhagen regressions, step ups, split squats in tolerable range, sled, cycling.
- Avoid painful deep flexion with rotation early; widen/narrow stance as comfortable.

### 12.6 Hip or knee replacement (cleared by surgeon)
- Respect surgeon's precautions (e.g. hip flexion/rotation limits for posterior approach early on). Strength training is beneficial. Avoid high impact/jumping. Use box squats to a safe height, step ups, sled, bike, leg curls, bridges.

### 12.7 Osteopenia / osteoporosis
- Principles (LIFTMOR, Royal Osteoporosis Society guidance): progressive strength training and appropriate impact are recommended. Progress to heavier loads under supervision. Good spinal position. Posture (thoracic extension) work.
- Use: deadlift variations (trap bar/KB from blocks, neutral spine), goblet/SSB squats, overhead press (landmine first), rows, back extensions (to neutral), carries, step ups, heel drops → small hops → jump drops progression where appropriate and cleared, balance training (falls prevention).
- Avoid: loaded spinal flexion (sit ups, crunches with load), loaded twisting with flexion, Jefferson curls, toe touches under load, high fall risk exercises. If vertebral fracture history → medical clearance and conservative progressions.

### 12.8 Menopause (peri and post)
- Principles (Sims, LIFTMOR): prioritise strength with heavier loads over time (progress to 4 to 8 rep work), power and impact (progressively), short sprint intervals over lots of long moderate cardio, recovery, protein, sleep.
- Symptoms (hot flushes, poor sleep, joint aches, low energy) → use traffic light; amber days reduce volume not frequency.
- Pelvic floor: avoid excessive breath holding with high abdominal pressure if symptoms (leaking, heaviness); breathe out on exertion; refer to a women's health physio if symptomatic. Jumping progressions only if no symptoms.
- Include balance and bone loading.

### 12.9 Hypertension (controlled)
- Avoid prolonged breath holding/Valsalva; exhale on effort; moderate loads (RPE ≤7 to 8), avoid sustained isometric max efforts and inverted positions if uncontrolled. Aerobic training helps. Uncontrolled → medical clearance first.

### 12.10 Type 2 diabetes / prediabetes
- Strength training and walking after meals improve glucose control. Check feet, hydration; carry quick sugars if on insulin/sulfonylureas.

### 12.11 Pregnancy and postnatal
- Requires GP/midwife clearance and pre/postnatal knowledge. Avoid supine lying for long periods after the first trimester, avoid high fall risk, avoid heavy breath holding, watch for diastasis and pelvic floor symptoms; postnatal women should ideally see a women's health physio before high impact. Flag for qualified coach.

### 12.12 Arthritis (general)
- "Motion is lotion": regular strength and movement reduce pain. Adjust range and load on flare days (amber/red). Warm up longer.

### 12.13 Former athletes (40+)
- Often stronger than their tissues can currently tolerate and prone to doing too much. Rebuild tissue tolerance first (Block 1), cap RPE at 8 for the first 8 weeks, prioritise mobility, single leg work and power reintroduction.

## SECTION 13. PROGRAMME TEMPLATES

### 13.1 28 day kickstart (all front end versions)
- 3 sessions/week minimum.
- Weeks 1 to 2: Foundation. Learn the patterns at G1 to G2, 2 to 3 sets of 10 to 12 at RPE 6 to 7, tempo 3-1-1-0, lots of coaching, teach bracing and breathing, establish carries and conditioning baselines.
- Weeks 3 to 4: add a set or reps, small load increases, first progression jumps for those ready, introduce power at G1 to G2 (med ball throws), finishers more challenging.
- Day 28: retest key screens and a conditioning test (e.g. 500m row, sit to stand, plank, carries) → show improvements → recommit conversation (back end).

### 13.2 Teams 3 day full body (intermediate, Block 2)
**Day A**
- Warm up: Lower A
- Core/Power: A1 med ball scoop toss 3×5 / A2 dead bug 3×8 each
- B1 SSB squat 4×6 to 8 @ 3-1-1-0, RPE 7 to 8 / B2 chest supported DB row 4×10
- C1 DB RDL 3×10 / C2 half kneeling landmine press 3×8 each / C3 Pallof press 3×10 each
- Finisher: sled push 6 × 20m
**Day B**
- Warm up: Upper A
- Core/Power: A1 KB swing 4×10 / A2 side plank 3×20 to 30s each
- B1 DB bench (neutral) 4×8 to 10 / B2 RFESS 4×8 each
- C1 band assisted pull up or lat pulldown 3×8 to 10 / C2 hip thrust 3×10 / C3 face pull 3×15
- Finisher: farmers carry 4 × 30m + assault bike 4 × 15s sprint
**Day C**
- Warm up: Full body
- Core/Power: A1 med ball rotational throw 3×5 each / A2 bird dog 3×6 each
- B1 trap bar or KB deadlift 4×6 / B2 TRX row 4×12
- C1 DB reverse lunge 3×8 each / C2 push up (level) 3× total reps / C3 suitcase carry 3 × 20m each
- Finisher: row 5 × 250m, 1 min rest

### 13.3 Group session template (mixed ability, 45 min)
Format example: "Strength + Engine"
- Warm up (10): bike/ski/row 2 min, lunge matrix, inchworm + WGS, band pull aparts, goblet squat prying.
- Block 1 (12): 4 rounds — A1 Squat level choice 8 to 10 reps / A2 Row level choice 10 to 12 reps / A3 Dead bug 8 each. Rest 60s.
- Block 2 (12): 3 rounds — B1 Hinge level choice 10 / B2 Push level choice 8 to 12 / B3 Carry 30m.
- Finisher (8): 4 × (40s work / 20s rest) rotating: bike, wall ball (level), sled push, ski.
- Cool down (3).
Each exercise slot lists G1 to G4 options (use the grade ladder at the top of each File 03 category).

### 13.4 Golf programme (2 to 3 days/week)
Principles: hip rotation (IR/ER) and T spine mobility, rotational power from the ground up (Gambetta), anti rotation core, single leg stability (lead leg), grip, glute strength, shoulder health, and avoiding loaded spinal twisting with flexion.
- Warm up: 90/90 hip switches, open book, T spine rotations in lunge, hip CARs, band pull aparts.
- Power: med ball rotational scoop toss and shot put throw (3×5 each side), lateral bounds (Teams only, G3+, screened; never in groups).
- Strength: trap bar/KB deadlift, RFESS, single leg RDL, landmine press, TRX/chest supported row, Pallof press, Copenhagen side plank, cable woodchop (controlled), farmers carry.
- Mobility finish: hip IR stretch, thread the needle, lat stretch.
- Off season: build strength (Block 2/3 loading). In season: maintain 1 to 2 shorter sessions, keep power, reduce soreness inducing eccentric volume before rounds.

### 13.5 Seniors programme (65+ or low capacity)
Principles: balance and gait every session, floor to stand practice, strength still progressive (just starting lower), power with safe tools (fast concentric on sit to stand, med ball throws, sled), carries, confidence.
- Warm up: seated/standing march, arm circles, sit to stand ×10, heel to toe walk, side steps, calf raises with support.
- Strength: sit to stand (box height regression), TRX assisted squat, step ups (low), wall/incline push ups, TRX/cable rows, KB deadlift from blocks, glute bridges, supported split squat.
- Power: fast sit to stand (stand up as fast as possible, lower slowly), med ball chest pass, sled push.
- Balance: tandem stance, single leg holds near support, stepping drills, clock reaches.
- Floor transfers: half kneeling to stand, get up progressions.
- Carries: farmers carry light to moderate.
- Conditioning: bike, rower steady intervals.

### 13.6 Fat loss emphasis (Cosgrove hierarchy)
Nutrition first (deficit, protein ~1.6 to 2.2 g/kg target bodyweight, coaching), 3 strength sessions (keep loads heavy enough to maintain muscle), 2 to 3 metabolic finishers/intervals, daily steps. Don't turn every session into cardio.

### 13.7 Muscle / strength emphasis
Higher weekly set volume (12 to 16 per muscle), accumulation/intensification blocks, double progression, longer rests on main lifts, sufficient protein and calories.

## SECTION 15. COACHING LANGUAGE AND CUEING PRINCIPLES

- One cue at a time. External cues (focus on the environment) often work better than internal ones: "push the floor away" beats "contract your quads".
- Teach in this order: position → breath/brace → movement → speed.
- Praise specific things. Correct one thing per set.
- Explain the why in one sentence ("this keeps your knees healthy for skiing next year"). Members who understand why adhere better.
- Celebrate PBs, level ups and retest improvements publicly (with permission). This builds the environment Optimal sells.
